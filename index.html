Research

My work sits between reinforcement learning in simulation and the decoding of biological signals. Both halves ask the same question from opposite ends: what has to be true about a skill, or about an intention, for it to still mean something once the body carrying it out is not the one it came from.

Cross-Embodiment Humanoid Skill Transfer

Isaac Lab · 2026–present

A locomotion skill learned on a Unitree G1 runs zero-shot on a NAO that never trained on it — nine times lighter and 2.3 times shorter. The skill crosses as a contract rather than as joint angles: the short future of a 27-number state describing what the body does, made dimensionless with its own leg length, mass and gravity.

Each skill's generator is trained once by flow matching on the source robot's rollouts; each body's estimator and tracker are trained once on procedurally generated plans and never see a transferred skill. So N skills on M bodies cost N + M trainings instead of N × M.

192 of 192 episodes complete without a fall per body and skill, at 1.00–1.14× the teacher's speed. On the NAO the zero-shot tracker stays closer to the teacher's gait (0.141–0.185 deviation) than a tracker trained on the skill itself (0.346–0.674) or joint-angle imitation (0.195–0.307). A task held out of every generator and tracker still runs: 288/288 on the NAO with cross-fitted plans.

All results are in simulation — no physical robot has walked with this. One source body and one target body, not arbitrary morphologies.

Musculoskeletal Hand Control from Motor Units

MuJoCo / MyoSuite · 2026–present

Decoding individual motor units from high-density surface EMG in order to drive a simulated musculoskeletal hand (MyoSuite's MyoHand). The open problem: decomposition methods lose track of motor units exactly when the limb moves quickly — which is precisely when an interface has to remain usable.

Building on the published adaptive method of Mendez Guerra, Barsakcioglu and Farina (2024), I added a closed-loop context head that decides which detected peaks are true discharges and feeds those decisions back into the adaptation.

On a held-out subject, evaluated once with every component frozen: it recovers 87.4% of discharges where the original method recovers 76.9% (+10.5, 95% cluster-bootstrap [7.6, 14.0]), and 78.6% against 60.5% during the fastest ramps. Units tracked at ≥90% agreement rise from 48.6% to 68.1%, with precision and recall improving together.

Ongoing research. The recordings are simulated HD-sEMG, which is why the true discharges are known and every decoded spike can be scored. The hand is not yet driven by the decoded motor units — that is the next stage of the project.

Safe Autonomous Catheter Navigation

Isaac Lab · 2026–present

A physics-based endovascular navigation research environment: an 81-body, 80-DOF flexible catheter advanced through vascular geometry with contact-force sensing. Wall-contact force is treated as a constraint rather than another weighted term in the reward, so the budget is stated in units a clinician can argue with instead of being hidden inside a tuned penalty weight. The cost is emitted on a channel structurally separate from the reward.

Phase 1 — environment construction. There is no reinforcement learning yet: _get_rewards() returns zeros by construction, and a test enforces that separation.

CAGE-NAS — Certified Functional Descent

Inria Saclay, équipe TAU · 2026

Uses functional-gradient approximations in the tangent space of the network to certify when to keep training parametrically and when to perform a function-preserving expansion, so capacity grows only once the current representation can no longer certify a functional descent step.

Grown architectures land above the 99.8th percentile of 76,646 exhaustively enumerated alternatives, with 0.9575 ± 0.0038 test accuracy over four seeds on the principal synthetic benchmark.
